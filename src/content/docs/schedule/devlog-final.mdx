### Final Devlog 

#### PhD Reels Development Process

Motivation: Short-form media has taken over the internet and left attention spans as a casualty. Students struggle to stay focused when a quick scroll and a dopamine rush are in their pockets. Many apps work to limit screen time by restricting apps, allowing students to stay more focused; however, this approach still relies on the willpower of students to not disable the helping app. What if instead of working to fix attention spans, we admit defeat and turn our school work into short-form media?
Our solution is to transform your online reading into reels with entertaining videos playing behind the text. 

Research into similar projects: https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/forest-stay-focused-be-pr/kjacjjdnoddnpbbcjilcajfhhbdhkpgk?pli=1
Forest: stay focused, be present
Forest works to keep users focused by rewarding them when they avoid distractions sites. 

https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/subway-surfers-screen-rea/jcijfneifjnhbgahlokgkmpcnocgpegd 

The subway surfers screen reader takes a similar approach to this project and allows users to turn their productive work into short, understandable reels.
<img src="/dig345-devlog/competition.png" alt="Competition research image" width="400" />

Different from the subway surfers screen reader, the project is a website instead of a chrome extension. The decision was made to simplify the development process. The idea is also that it is easier to turn a working website into a chrome extension than to build a chrome extension from scratch if I ever decide to make the project a chrome extension.

After user responses to the initial designs I added a horizontal scroll bar that showed thumbnails of all the video options. This way users could see what the videos were before clicking on them. Additional user feedback led to creating a pause/play and reset button. These buttons replaced the generate reel button while the reel was playing.
